HOW TO READ THIS

An engineering screen you can inspect

01 / Start with the gap

Headroom(t) = connection − adjusted host load(t). Reserve 5% of that gap, then size to the chosen fraction of fully served UTC days. A separate 100% coverage row preserves the strict minimum-gap baseline.

02 / Shift, don’t create, energy

Battery power limits the peak it can cover. Usable energy limits its duration. Recharge stays inside the same connection headroom. End-of-profile stored energy must support a repeatable cycle.

03 / Separate facility and IT

IT kW = facility kW ÷ peak overhead factor. This is power-supported potential, subject to cooling, space, fiber, utility approval, permits, and redundancy design.

04 / Make evidence visible

Synthetic or incomplete profiles are flagged. A single year does not guarantee future availability. Existing solar or battery operation must not be double-counted as dependable spare capacity.

Assumptions, limits & sources

All capacity calculations use interval-average loads. They do not validate inrush, harmonics, protection, equipment derating, battery outages, or instantaneous demand. Battery dispatch maximizes technically supportable constant load with greedy charging; it does not optimize tariff cost or battery size. Battery size is coupled to the resulting IT capacity. The 100% coverage row uses that same installed battery. Daily-coverage sizing uses a load-following controller, not a global optimization of which days to sacrifice. It reports the largest supported load found under that policy. Historical headroom coverage excludes grid outages, equipment failures, and other causes of downtime. The full installed battery is priced in each scenario. An extra battery can reduce project value.

The tariff is a simplified flat energy charge plus incremental UTC calendar-month peak demand. No time-of-use windows, ratchets, coincident peaks, taxes, supplier renegotiation, or utility tariff changes. Uploaded profiles must cover complete UTC days, and one continuous year aligned to a UTC month boundary to enable financial outputs. Short samples only establish coverage within that sample. Occupancy changes revenue only; the full target load is consumed except during explicitly simulated power shortfalls. Contractual revenue assumes the selected coverage is accepted without SLA credits; change revenue assumptions before commercial use. Peak overhead is constant; battery HVAC/auxiliary consumption is not separately modeled.

Financial outputs assume constant annual cash, end-year payments for residual budget, no terminal value, tax, incentives, financing, or escalation. Battery retention is a conservative capacity derating, with a separately specified refresh expense. Stress-test loads, costs, outages, and battery power derating before investment.

2025 ATB: commercial battery storage supports the illustrative 85% round-trip efficiency. SAM: chronological battery dispatch. These sources do not validate the scenario budgets or illustrative commercial inputs.
